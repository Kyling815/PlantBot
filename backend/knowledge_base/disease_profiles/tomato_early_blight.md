# Tomato — Early Blight (*Alternaria solani*)

## Overview
Early blight is one of the most common fungal diseases of tomatoes, caused by the fungus *Alternaria solani*. It can cause significant yield losses if left untreated.

## Symptoms
- **Dark brown concentric rings** forming a "target" or "bullseye" pattern on older leaves
- Yellow chlorotic halo surrounding each lesion
- Lesions typically 1–2 cm in diameter
- Stems may show dark, sunken cankers (collar rot)
- Fruit infection: dark, leathery, sunken lesions near the stem

## Causal Agent
- **Pathogen**: *Alternaria solani* (fungus)
- **Type**: Foliar blight / collar rot
- **Spread**: Airborne spores, rain splash, infected seed, soil debris

## Conditions Favoring Disease
- Warm temperatures (24–29°C)
- High humidity (> 80%)
- Extended leaf wetness periods
- Drought-stressed or nutrient-deficient plants

## Disease Cycle
1. Fungus overwinters in infected plant debris in the soil
2. Spores released during warm, wet weather
3. Spores land on leaves and germinate in the presence of moisture
4. New lesions produce spores within 7–10 days
5. Disease spreads upward through the canopy
