# Potato — Late Blight (*Phytophthora infestans*)

## Overview
Late blight is a devastating oomycete disease caused by *Phytophthora infestans*, historically responsible for the Irish Potato Famine (1840s). It spreads rapidly under cool, wet conditions.

## Symptoms
- **Water-soaked, pale green lesions** on leaves that quickly turn brown/black
- White, cottony sporulation on the underside of leaves in humid conditions
- Rapid collapse of entire leaflets and stems
- Tubers: reddish-brown rot beneath the skin; foul smell

## Causal Agent
- **Pathogen**: *Phytophthora infestans* (oomycete / water mould)
- **Type**: Foliar and tuber blight
- **Spread**: Wind-dispersed sporangia, infected tubers, irrigation water

## Conditions Favoring Disease
- Cool temperatures (10–20°C)
- Prolonged leaf wetness (> 10 hours)
- High relative humidity (> 90%)
- Dense canopy with poor air circulation

## Disease Cycle
1. Pathogen overwinters in infected tubers or plant debris
2. Sporangia produced and dispersed by wind/rain during cool, wet periods
3. Infection occurs when sporangia germinate on wet leaf surfaces
4. New lesions produce millions of sporangia within 4–5 days
5. Entire fields can be destroyed within 2 weeks under ideal conditions
