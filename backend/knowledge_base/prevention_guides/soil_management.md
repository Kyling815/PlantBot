# Soil Management for Disease Prevention

## Soil Health and Disease
Healthy soil supports beneficial microorganisms that naturally suppress many plant pathogens. Poor soil management depletes these populations and creates conditions that favour disease.

## Key Practices

### 1. Crop Rotation
- **Never plant the same crop family** in the same spot for 2–3 years
- Rotation breaks the pathogen lifecycle by removing the host
- Rotation schedule example:
  - Year 1: Tomatoes / Potatoes (Solanaceae)
  - Year 2: Legumes (Beans, Peas)
  - Year 3: Brassicas (Cabbage, Broccoli)
  - Year 4: Root vegetables (Carrots, Onions)

### 2. Organic Matter
- Add compost (5–10 cm layer) each season
- Compost introduces beneficial microbes that compete with pathogens
- Improves drainage and aeration

### 3. pH Management
| Pathogen Type | Favoured pH | Corrective Action |
|---|---|---|
| Most fungi | 5.5–6.5 | Lime to raise pH |
| Club root (*Plasmodiophora*) | < 7.0 | Lime heavily |
| Common scab of potato | > 5.5 | Lower pH with sulphur |

Target pH: **6.0–6.8** for most vegetables

### 4. Drainage
- Ensure raised beds or ridges for waterlogging-prone soils
- Add horticultural grit or coarse sand to heavy clay soils
- Avoid compaction by not walking on planting beds

### 5. Solarisation
- Cover bare soil with clear plastic during summer (4–6 weeks)
- Solar heat kills many soil-borne pathogens and weed seeds
- Effective against *Fusarium*, *Verticillium*, and nematodes

### 6. Biofungicides
- *Trichoderma*-based products — colonise root zone and suppress pathogens
- *Bacillus subtilis* — effective against many fungal root diseases
- Apply at transplanting or as a soil drench
