# Pruning Practices for Disease Prevention

## Why Pruning Helps
Proper pruning improves air circulation through the plant canopy, reducing the humidity and leaf wetness that fungi and bacteria need to thrive.

## General Pruning Principles

1. **Use sharp, clean tools** — Dull blades crush tissue; dirty blades spread disease
2. **Disinfect between plants** — 70% isopropyl alcohol or 10% bleach solution
3. **Make clean cuts** — Cut at a 45° angle just above a leaf node or bud
4. **Remove the 4 Ds**: Dead, Damaged, Diseased, and Deranged (crossing) branches
5. **Dispose properly** — Bag and remove (or burn) pruned material; do not compost diseased tissue

## Tomato-Specific Pruning
- Remove **suckers** (side shoots in leaf axils) to maintain a single main stem
- Defoliate the bottom 30 cm of the plant to reduce soil splash infection
- Remove leaves that are touching the ground
- Thin the canopy to allow sunlight penetration

## Apple Tree Pruning
- Prune during **dormancy** (late winter) to reduce disease spread
- Open up the centre of the tree ("open-vase" or "central-leader" form)
- Remove fire blight cankers at least 30 cm below visible infection

## When to Prune
| Crop | Best Time | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Tomatoes | Continuously throughout season | Hot, sunny midday |
| Peppers | Remove diseased tissue as needed | Wet weather |
| Fruit trees | Late winter / early spring | Active growth periods |
| Grapes | Dormant season | After bud break |
