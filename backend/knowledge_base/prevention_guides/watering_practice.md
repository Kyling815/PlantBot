# Watering Practices for Disease Prevention

## Why Watering Matters
Incorrect watering creates the moisture conditions that most fungal and bacterial diseases need to thrive. Most foliar diseases require free water (dew, rain, irrigation droplets) on the leaf surface to infect.

## Golden Rules

### ✅ DO
- **Water at the base** of the plant (drip irrigation is ideal)
- **Water in the morning** so leaves dry out during the day
- **Water deeply and infrequently** to encourage deep root development
- **Monitor soil moisture** — use a finger-test or moisture meter
- **Use mulch** (straw, compost) to retain soil moisture and reduce splash

### ❌ DON'T
- **Avoid overhead sprinklers** — they wet leaves and spread disease spores
- **Don't water in the evening** — leaves remain wet overnight, ideal for infection
- **Don't overwater** — waterlogged roots are susceptible to rot
- **Don't let water pool** around plant bases

## Irrigation Systems
| Type | Disease Risk | Best For |
|---|---|---|
| Drip irrigation | Very low ✅ | Tomatoes, peppers, cucumbers |
| Soaker hoses | Low ✅ | Row crops |
| Sprinklers | High ❌ | Lawns only |
| Flood irrigation | Moderate ⚠️ | Field crops with good drainage |

## Seasonal Adjustments
- **Spring**: Reduce watering frequency if rainfall is high
- **Summer**: Water every 2–3 days in hot weather; early morning only
- **Autumn**: Reduce as temperatures drop; allow soil to dry between waterings
- **Winter**: Minimal watering; ensure good drainage
